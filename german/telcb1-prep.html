Regeln, die Punkte bringen

Spend minutes where the points are

One Lesen-2 question is worth 5 points, a Sprachbaustein 1,5. Give Lesen 1 and 2 your calmest attention and move fast through the gaps.

Questions follow the text

In Lesen 2 and Hören 2 the items run in the same order as the text. If you find the answer to item 3, item 4 comes after it.

Same words are a warning

The right option usually paraphrases the text. An option that repeats a word from the text but changes the meaning is the classic trap.

Watch the small words

nicht, kein, nur, erst, noch, schon, alle, immer, nie. One of them can turn a statement from richtig to falsch.

Eliminate, then cross out

In every matching task, solve the sure ones first and cross out what you used. The last items get easier with fewer options left.

Read before you listen

Use every pause in Hören to read the next statements and mark the key word: a time, a place, a price, a change.

Four Leitpunkte beat a long letter

In Schreiben, each guiding point you skip costs more than any grammar mistake. Write two or three sentences per point and stop.

Talk with your partner, not at the examiner

The oral exam rewards interaction. Ask questions, react, agree, disagree with a reason, and summarise.

Fill the answer sheet as you go

Only the Antwortbogen counts. Don't plan on extra minutes to copy answers over at the end.

Guess smart, never blank

Richtig/falsch is a coin flip at worst. In matching tasks, guess among the options you have not used yet.

Deine 90 Minuten

Lesen and Sprachbausteine share one 90-minute block, and you choose the order. Here is a split that protects the 5-point items. Run it during mock tests so the pacing feels normal on the day.

Lesen

Three parts, 75 points, inside the 90-minute block. In every part you are searching for something specific. You rarely need to understand every word.

Teil 1

Globalverstehen

5 texts · 10 headlines5 P. per item25 P.target 15 min

Each short text gets one headline. Five headlines are left over, and they are written to tempt you.

  1. Read all 10 headlines first (one minute). Underline the key idea in each: who, and what happens.
  2. Read each text and ask one question: what is this text about as a whole? The first and the last sentence usually tell you.
  3. Match the obvious ones, then cross out the headlines you used.
  4. For the rest, check the headline against the whole text, not against one sentence in it.
Fallen
  • A headline repeats a word from the text but claims something the text never says.
  • A headline fits a detail, not the main idea.
  • A headline says the opposite: mehr instead of weniger, teurer instead of kostenlos.
Trick
  • Summarise each text in three German words before you look at the headlines again.
  • If two headlines both fit, the right one covers the whole text; the other covers one sentence.
Teil 2

Detailverstehen

1 text · 5 questions a/b/c5 P. per item25 P.target 20 min

A newspaper-style article. The questions check specific statements, and they come in the order of the text.

  1. Read the questions and all options first. Underline what each one is really asking.
  2. Skim the text once for the overall topic.
  3. For each question, find the matching passage. It comes after the passage of the previous question.
  4. Read that passage word by word and rule out options until one is left.
Fallen
  • An option uses exact words from the text but from a different paragraph.
  • An option is true in general, but the text doesn't say it.
  • An option is too strong: immer, alle, nur, nie.
Trick
  • The correct option is almost always a paraphrase: different words, same meaning.
  • Mark each option as "says the text", "contradicts the text" or "not in the text". Only the first one scores.
Teil 3

Selektives Verstehen

10 situations · 12 ads2,5 P. per item25 P.target 20 min

People are looking for something, and you scan ads to find what fits. Each ad can be used once, and when no ad fits a situation you mark x.

  1. Read the situations first and underline the hard criteria: who (children, beginners), when (weekend, evening), where, how much (höchstens 100 €), and what is excluded (nicht laut, ohne Auto).
  2. Scan the ads for those criteria, not for the topic. Three ads may be about bikes; only one costs less than 100 €.
  3. An ad must meet every criterion. One mismatch rules it out.
  4. Cross out used ads. If nothing fits all criteria, mark x.
Fallen
  • The topic fits, but one detail doesn't: wrong day, wrong age group, too expensive, indoors instead of outdoors.
  • An ad mentions the same object (a dog, a bike) for a different need.
Trick
  • Expect few x answers. If you are marking several, check the criteria again.
  • This part pays 2,5 per item. If time is short, do it fast and keep minutes for Teil 1 and 2.

Sprachbausteine

Twenty gaps, 1,5 points each, 30 points in total. The grammar part tests the same handful of structures again and again, so this is where drilling pays off most per hour of study.

Teil 1

Grammatik

letter · 10 gaps · a/b/c1,5 P. per item15 P.target 10 min
  1. Read the whole sentence, including what comes after the gap. The answer is often decided by the words after it.
  2. Name the grammar being tested: connector and verb position, case after a preposition, fixed preposition of a verb, relative pronoun, reflexive pronoun, tense or passive.
  3. Check the verb position after the gap. Verb at the end means weil/obwohl/dass; verb right after means deshalb/trotzdem; normal order means denn/aber.
  4. If you are unsure after 30 seconds, guess and move on.

What comes up again and again

  • Konnektoren: weil / denn / deshalb, obwohl / trotzdem, dass / ob, wenn / als, damit / um … zu
  • Verben mit Präposition: sich freuen auf/über, warten auf, sich interessieren für, teilnehmen an, sich kümmern um
  • Relativpronomen: der, den, dem, dessen, denen, often with a preposition
  • Präpositionen + Kasus: seit / vor, am / im / um, in + Dativ oder Akkusativ
  • Konjunktiv II and Passiv: könnten, wäre, hätte, würde; wird / wurde + Partizip
  • Pronomen: mir / mich, ihm / ihn, Ihnen, and reflexive mich / mir

All of these are explained on one screen each under Grammatik.

Teil 2

Lexik

text · 10 gaps · 15 words1,5 P. per item15 P.target 10 min
  1. Look at the neighbours of each gap and decide what kind of word fits: article before it means a noun; zu plus gap means a verb; a gap before a noun may be a preposition.
  2. Read the 15 words and sort them roughly: prepositions, verbs, nouns, connectors.
  3. Fill the sure gaps first and cross out each word you use. Five words stay unused.
  4. Read the full text once more with your words in it.

Hören

About 30 minutes, 75 points, paced by the recording. Two of the three parts pay 5 points per item, so every statement you read in advance is worth the effort.

Teil 1

Globalverstehen

5 radio texts · heard once5 P. per item25 P.

News, weather, short reports. You hear each text only once, and each has one statement about its main message.

  1. In the pause, read the statement and underline the core claim.
  2. Listen for the gist. Ignore numbers and names unless the statement depends on them.
  3. Decide right after the text ends and turn your attention to the next statement. There is no second chance, so don't dwell.
Teil 2

Detailverstehen

1 conversation · heard twice2,5 P. per item25 P.

A longer radio interview or conversation with 10 statements in the order of the recording.

  1. Read all ten statements in the pause and mark the key word in each.
  2. First listening: mark an answer for every statement, even a tentative one.
  3. Second listening: check only the ones you were unsure about.
Teil 3

Selektives Verstehen

5 announcements · heard twice5 P. per item25 P.

Station announcements, shop announcements, voicemail. Each statement asks about one specific piece of information.

  1. Read the statement and decide what you are listening for: a time, a platform, a price, a date.
  2. Listen for the change. Announcements exist because something is different today: ausnahmsweise, leider, statt, verschoben, fällt aus.
  3. Use the second listening to confirm the exact detail.
Fallen
  • Numbers that sound alike: fünfzehn / fünfzig, dreizehn / dreißig; times like halb zwei = 1:30, Viertel vor drei = 2:45.
  • A plan that changes mid-text: eigentlich … aber jetzt.
  • The statement mentions what you hear, but negated or limited: nur, erst, nicht mehr, kein.
Signalwörter
  • Contrast: aber, doch, jedoch, allerdings, dagegen, stattdessen
  • Change: ausnahmsweise, leider, verschoben, abgesagt, fällt aus
  • Limits: nur, erst, schon, noch, kaum, sogar

Hörtraining

Practice texts read aloud by your device's German voice. Like the exam, each one plays once or twice only, so read the statement before you press play.

Schreiben

One e-mail in 30 minutes, worth 45 points. It is either informal (to a friend, du) or semi-formal (to someone you know or a company, Sie), and it must answer four guiding points, the Leitpunkte.

How it is scored

KriteriumWhat raters look atHow you collect the points
I · AufgabeAre all four Leitpunkte covered with fitting content?One paragraph per point, two or three sentences each, in the order of the task.
II · GestaltungRegister (du or Sie throughout), greeting and closing, logical order, connectorsBetreff, matching Anrede and Gruß, four or five connectors (weil, deshalb, außerdem, trotzdem, dass).
III · RichtigkeitGrammar, word order, spelling. Do errors get in the way of understanding?Short sentences you are sure of, plus a five-minute error check at the end.

Each criterion is graded A to D. A clear, complete, slightly simple e-mail scores better than an ambitious one that skips a point.

Die 30-Minuten-Methode

  1. Minutes 0–5: plan. Decide du or Sie. Write 2–4 German keywords next to each Leitpunkt. Choose your Anrede and Gruß.
  2. Minutes 5–25: write. Betreff, Anrede, one opening sentence, then one paragraph per Leitpunkt in order, one closing sentence, Gruß. Aim for about 120–150 words; extra length earns nothing and adds mistakes.
  3. Minutes 25–30: check. Verb in second position? Verb at the end after weil/dass/wenn? Nouns capitalised? Sie/Ihnen/Ihr capitalised when formal? Small letter after the comma of the Anrede?

Point killers
  • Skipping or barely touching one Leitpunkt.
  • Switching between du and Sie.
  • Copying whole sentences from the task. Rephrase them in your own words.
  • No Anrede or no Gruß, or a formal Gruß in a letter to a friend.
Safe moves
  • Open each paragraph so the rater sees which point it answers: Zu deiner Frage nach …, Außerdem möchte ich …
  • Give every point a reason with weil or deshalb. That covers content and grammar at once.
  • Learn one template per register by heart and adapt it.

Sprechen

About 15 minutes in pairs, after 20 minutes of preparation. Two examiners listen. Teil 2 is worth the most. Across all three parts they reward the same thing: a real conversation with your partner.

Use the 20 minutes
  • Write keywords, not sentences. Reading out full sentences sounds stiff and loses the eye contact with your partner.
  • Teil 2: note the topic of your material, 2 opinions with reasons, 1 example from your life or country, 2 questions for your partner.
  • Teil 3: next to each task point, note one suggestion and one alternative.
Don't
  • Recite a memorised monologue. Examiners hear it at once.
  • Talk only to the examiners. Turn to your partner.
  • Stay silent when stuck. Say Moment, wie sagt man das … and describe the word.
Teil 1

Einander kennenlernen

about 3 minno material needed

You and your partner get to know each other. Ask as many questions as you answer, and react to what you hear. Ask early whether you use du: Wollen wir uns duzen?

Reagieren

  • Ach, wirklich? Das ist ja interessant!
  • Das geht mir genauso.
  • Und wie ist das bei dir?
  • Wie lange machst du das schon?
  • Erzähl mal, wie ist es dort?

Nachfragen

  • Kannst du das bitte noch einmal sagen?
  • Wie meinst du das?
  • Was gefällt dir daran besonders?
  • Und was möchtest du nach der Prüfung machen?
Teil 2

Über ein Thema sprechen

about 6 minhighest weight of the three parts

You get material on a topic, such as a short text or statements. Present the main point, give your opinion with reasons, bring in your own experience, then discuss it with your partner.

  1. Topic: In meinem Text geht es um … Der Text sagt, dass …
  2. Opinion with a reason: Ich finde, dass …, weil … Meiner Meinung nach …
  3. Experience or home country: Bei uns in … ist das anders: … Ich habe selbst erlebt, dass …
  4. Hand over: Was meinst du dazu? Wie ist das in deinem Land? Then respond to what your partner says.

Zustimmen

  • Da hast du recht.
  • Das sehe ich genauso.
  • Ja, genau, und außerdem …

Widersprechen

  • Das stimmt schon, aber …
  • Das sehe ich ein bisschen anders, denn …
  • Ich bin nicht sicher, ob das immer so ist.
Teil 3

Gemeinsam etwas planen

about 5–6 mintask sheet with points to settle

You plan something together, such as a party, a trip or a visit. The sheet lists the points to settle. Cover every point, make suggestions, react to your partner's ideas and agree on a plan.

  1. Go through the points in the order on the sheet.
  2. For each point: suggestion, reaction, decision. Disagree politely at least once, with a reason and an alternative.
  3. Split the jobs: Wer kümmert sich um …? Ich kann … übernehmen.
  4. Close with a summary: Also, fassen wir zusammen: …

Vorschlagen

  • Wollen wir …?
  • Ich schlage vor, dass wir …
  • Wie wäre es mit …?
  • Wir könnten doch …
  • Was hältst du von …?

Annehmen · Ablehnen

  • Gute Idee! / Einverstanden.
  • Das finde ich nicht so gut, weil … Vielleicht …?
  • Am Samstag kann ich leider nicht. Geht es auch am Sonntag?
  • Das ist ein bisschen teuer. Was hältst du von …?

Grammatik

The structures the exam actually tests, one card each. Read a card, then drill it under Sprachbausteine with the matching filter.

Konnektoren und Verbposition

The single most tested point in Sprachbausteine. Choose by what happens to the verb after the connector.

Position 0 · order staysPosition 1 · verb comes nextNebensatz · verb to the end
und, aber, oder, denn, sondern deshalb, deswegen, darum, trotzdem, dann, danach, außerdem, sonst weil, da, obwohl, dass, ob, wenn, als, damit, bevor, nachdem, während, bis, falls
Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin krank. Ich bin krank, deshalb bleibe ich zu Hause. Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich krank bin.

Paare: nicht nur … sondern auch · entweder … oder · sowohl … als auch · weder … noch · je … desto · zwar … aber

wenn / als: als for one event or period in the past; wenn for repeated events, the present and the future. dass / ob: ob replaces a yes/no question. damit / um … zu: um … zu needs an infinitive.

Präpositionen und Kasus

Akkusativ: durch, für, gegen, ohne, um

Remember it as FUDGO: für, um, durch, gegen, ohne.

Dativ: aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu (+ gegenüber)

Sing aus-bei-mit-nach-seit-von-zu to the tune of the Blue Danube waltz. It sticks.

Wechselpräpositionen: an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen · wo? → Dativ, wohin? → Akkusativ

Das Bild hängt an der Wand. · Ich hänge das Bild an die Wand.

Genitiv: wegen, trotz, während, statt

After seit, vor and in (time), plural nouns take Dativ -n: seit drei Jahren.

Ask about things with wo(r)+Präp. (Worauf wartest du?) and point back with da(r)+Präp. (Ich warte darauf.). For people: Auf wen wartest du? Ich warte auf ihn.

Konjunktiv II

würde + Infinitiv · hätte · wäre · könnte · müsste · sollte

  • Polite request: Könnten Sie mir bitte … schicken?
  • Polite wish: Ich hätte gern … / Ich würde gern wissen, ob …
  • Unreal: Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich öfter reisen.
  • Advice: Du solltest früher anfangen.
  • In letters: Ich wäre Ihnen dankbar, wenn Sie …

Passiv

PräsensDie Heizung wird repariert.
PräteritumDas Paket wurde geliefert.

Common in notices and official letters, so it turns up in Lesen and Sprachbausteine.

Perfekt mit sein

Movement from A to B or a change of state, plus sein, bleiben, werden, passieren.

gehen, fahren, fliegen, kommen, laufen, umziehen, aufstehen, einschlafen, sterben, wachsen

Adjektivendungen

  • After der / die / das: -e in the nominative singular and in the accusative die / das; -en everywhere else.
  • After ein / kein / mein: the adjective shows the gender where the article can't: ein guter Kurs, ein gutes Buch, eine gute Idee; -en in Dativ and Genitiv.
  • No article: the ending of der/die/das: mit frischem Brot.

Wortschatz

The exam's texts come from a small world: offices, flats, jobs, doctors, shops, trains and free time. These cards cover the words and fixed phrases that carry those texts, plus the signal words that decide richtig or falsch. Cards you miss come back more often.

Prüfungstag

In the written exam

  • Start the 90-minute block with a time plan in your head, and look at the clock after every part.
  • Transfer answers to the Antwortbogen after each part, not at the end.
  • Before Hören starts, look at the first statements.
  • In Schreiben, tick off each Leitpunkt on the task sheet as you finish it.

In the oral exam

  • Greet your partner and the examiners. A calm first sentence helps.
  • If you didn't understand: Entschuldigung, können Sie die Frage bitte wiederholen?
  • Help your partner if they get stuck. It shows interaction and costs you nothing.
