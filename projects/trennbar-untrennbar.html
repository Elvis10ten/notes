Scope

Prefix verbs at a glance

German builds whole families of verbs by attaching a prefix to a base verb. With a separable verb (trennbar) the prefix flies to the end of a main clause. With an inseparable one (untrennbar) it stays welded on.

3 · Dual prefixes: sometimes one, sometimes the other

durch-hinter-über-um-unter-voll-wider-wieder-

Here the difference carries meaning. The rough tendency: concrete or spatial senses tend to separate, with the prefix stressed; figurative senses tend not to, with the stem stressed.

InfinitiveSeparable — prefix stressedInseparable — stem stressed
umfahrenIch fahre den Baum um. (knock over)Ich umfahre den Baum. (drive around)
übersetzenDer Fährmann setzt uns über. (ferry across)Sie übersetzt den Text. (translate)
durchschauenIch schaue die Unterlagen durch. (review)Ich durchschaue dich. (see through)
wiederholenIch hole das Buch wieder. (fetch back)Ich wiederhole den Satz. (repeat)
unterhaltenEr hält den Schirm unter. (hold under)Wir unterhalten uns. (converse)
umstellenIch stelle die Möbel um. (rearrange)Die Polizei umstellt das Haus. (surround)
umgehenEr geht gut mit Kindern um. (deal with)Er umgeht das Problem. (bypass)
übergehenWir gehen zum nächsten Punkt über. (move on)Man hat mich übergangen. (pass over)
The concrete-versus-figurative rule is a tendency, not a rule. umfahren already breaks it: the inseparable reading (um den Baum herumfahren) is itself spatial, while the separable one (knocking the tree over) is the one with the physical contact. A better cue for the inseparable readings: the object is directly affected or fully enclosed. Beyond that, stress and meaning have to be learned verb by verb — which is what the Meaning category in the practice deck is for.

5 · Where the ge- disappears

A separable verb slots ge- between prefix and stem — an-ge-rufen. Unless the stem itself cannot take a ge-: because it already starts with an inseparable prefix, or because it ends in -ieren.

6 · The listening test

When you are unsure, say the verb out loud. If the weight falls on the prefix, it separates. If it falls on the stem, it stays. That is the only test that works reliably for the dual prefixes — and in writing, some forms (subordinate clause, modal verb, future) are identical for both readings.
